The latest in West Queen Anne

November 12, 2026

Marshall Park, 1191 7th Ave. W, across the street from Parsons Garden, is a small patch of green with three benches and a view of the Sound. It holds a memorial to arts patron Betty Bowen, and several well-known Northwest artists left unsigned work cast into the concrete walkway. Seattle Parks

November 5, 2026

Kerry Park, at 211 W Highland Dr., is open 6 a.m. to 10 p.m. It looks over Elliott Bay and the Central City, with an occasional backdrop of Mount Rainier. At sunset visitors often line the wall as the city and the Sound begin to light up. Seattle Parks

October 29, 2026

West Queen Anne Playfield, 150 West Blaine Street, is in design for synthetic turf. Fields 1 and 2 and the soccer field go from grass to turf with new backstops and fencing ($6.8 million); upper Field 3 gets turf, lighting and dugouts with Queen Anne Little League, up to $3 million. Construction is planned for summer and fall 2027, with the fields closed, and completion in spring 2028. Seattle Parks

October 22, 2026

Queen Anne Pool, 1920 1st Avenue West, stays closed until fall 2027. It closed in November 2025 after losing about 400,000 gallons of water a month through underground inflow pipes; the permanent fix is part of the Citywide Pool Renovation Project. This fall Ballard Pool adds Saturday afternoon swims, and Evans and Medgar Evers pools add Sunday hours and early-morning lap swim. Winter 2027 lesson registration opens December 8 (December 1 for the Scholarship Program). Seattle Parks

October 17, 2026

The Queen Anne Community Center, 1901 1st Avenue West, has reached 60% design for its renovation, with permits submitted. Bidding is planned for the first quarter of 2027; the city has not set a construction start. The work replaces lighting, plumbing, heating and cooling, electrifies the building and reclaims old hallways and locker rooms, on $4.4 million plus $18.9 million from the Seattle Park District. Seattle Parks

Questions people ask

Is this a real estate newsletter?

No. It is a Queen Anne & Magnolia newsletter that happens to know the housing market. Three of the four sections are about living here. The fourth tells you what homes are selling for, because in Queen Anne & Magnolia that is news too.

Who writes it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publisher. Every item comes from Queen Anne & Magnolia's own public record (council agendas, permit notices, school district and transit updates, parks calendars) and links its source. It is researched and written with AI tools, and an editor reads every issue before it goes out.

How often, and is it really free?

Once a week, Thursday at 6 AM, about five minutes to read. Free, no paywall, unsubscribe in one click at the bottom of any issue.

Who owns it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publishing company. The newsletter is free, and no sponsor buys coverage. Our standards.

What happens with my address?

It is used for one thing: a monthly estimate of what your home would sell for today. It is shared with a local real estate brokerage under the same ownership as our publisher, which may contact you about your home, and is never sold or posted. Remove it any time by replying to any issue.
