The latest in North Queen Anne

November 5, 2026

David Rodgers Park, open 4 a.m. to 11:30 p.m. on the upper part of 1st Avenue W, has a play area renovated in 2019, three tennis courts and public restrooms. Its paths wind down to the Queen Anne Bowl. The park is named for David Rodgers (1864–1923), a shipfitter who built vessels quickly for the war effort. Seattle Parks

October 29, 2026

The Queen Anne Bowl Playfield, 2806 3rd Avenue West, takes its "bowl" from its first life as a gravel quarry. Today it is a playfield and track with soccer fields, open 4 AM to 11:30 PM. Seattle Parks

October 22, 2026

David Rodgers Park sits on a steep, sculpted hillside on upper 1st Avenue West. The play area, renovated in 2019, has nature-themed equipment and a panel listing when each state joined the Union; there are also three tennis courts and restrooms. Paths wind down grassy knolls to the Queen Anne Bowl. Seattle Parks

October 17, 2026

At the foot of West Ewing Street by the South Ship Canal Trail, a 74-foot drop shaft and an 8-foot tunnel under the canal to Fremont now wait on the rest of the Ship Canal Water Quality Project. The site is quiet apart from occasional inspection and restoration crews. When the storage tunnel starts working in 2027, it keeps an average of more than 75 million gallons of stormwater and sewage a year out of the Ship Canal, Salmon Bay and Lake Union. Seattle Public Utilities · September update

Questions people ask

Is this a real estate newsletter?

No. It is a Queen Anne & Magnolia newsletter that happens to know the housing market. Three of the four sections are about living here. The fourth tells you what homes are selling for, because in Queen Anne & Magnolia that is news too.

Who writes it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publisher. Every item comes from Queen Anne & Magnolia's own public record (council agendas, permit notices, school district and transit updates, parks calendars) and links its source. It is researched and written with AI tools, and an editor reads every issue before it goes out.

How often, and is it really free?

Once a week, Thursday at 6 AM, about five minutes to read. Free, no paywall, unsubscribe in one click at the bottom of any issue.

Who owns it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publishing company. The newsletter is free, and no sponsor buys coverage. Our standards.

What happens with my address?

It is used for one thing: a monthly estimate of what your home would sell for today. It is shared with a local real estate brokerage under the same ownership as our publisher, which may contact you about your home, and is never sold or posted. Remove it any time by replying to any issue.
